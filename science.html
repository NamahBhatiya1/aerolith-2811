The four techniques behind Aerolith, and how each one works.

Four techniques, one loop

Each technique feeds the next, so one waste stream becomes the input for another. Filter by what it is for.

Geopolymerisation

Quarry dust mixed with sodium silicate forms a binding material. The mixture is moulded and cured into eco-friendly bricks.

More detail

The binder forms by chemical reaction, so the plan is to mould and cure the bricks rather than fire them.

MICP treatment

Microbially induced calcium carbonate precipitation strengthens the cured bricks through natural calcium carbonate formation.

More detail

Microbes help calcium carbonate, the mineral in limestone, form inside the brick. It is a second strengthening step applied after curing.

Acoustic agglomeration

Ultrasound makes fine airborne dust particles clump into larger aggregates that are easier to collect and separate.

More detail

Sound waves make small particles collide and stick together. Larger aggregates are easier to filter or settle out than fine dust.

Mechanochemical carbon capture

Mechanical processing exposes reactive mineral surfaces in the dust so they can react with CO₂ and lock it into stable carbonates.

More detail

Magnesium oxide, where present or added, reacts with CO₂ to form magnesium carbonate. Filtration can then separate the carbonates; a MOF is an untested option.

How each technique works

Geopolymerisation

Sodium silicate is strongly alkaline. Mixed with quarry dust, it dissolves some of the reactive silica and alumina in the dust’s aluminosilicate minerals. The dissolved silicate and aluminate units then link together into a three-dimensional network of Si–O–Al bonds, with sodium ions balancing the charge. The network sets into a hard binder that holds the remaining grains together, and it cures at low temperature instead of in a kiln.

How much of the dust is reactive enough depends on its minerals, so this is one of the first things to test.

  • Dust + sodium silicate
  • Dissolution
  • Si–O–Al network
  • Hard binder
Inputs
Quarry dust, sodium silicate, water
Outputs
A hardened aluminosilicate binder, shaped as a brick
Purpose
Bind dust into a solid brick without firing

MICP

Sporosarcina pasteurii is a soil bacterium that makes the enzyme urease. Urease breaks urea into ammonium and carbonate ions. Where calcium ions are present, the carbonate combines with them to form calcium carbonate (CaCO₃). The bacterial cells give the crystals places to start growing, so the carbonate forms inside the brick’s pores and cracks, filling them and bridging neighbouring grains.

Geopolymer is very alkaline and bacteria need a tolerable pH, so we must test whether MICP works on these bricks and when to apply it.

  • Bacteria + urea + calcium
  • Urease
  • Carbonate + calcium
  • CaCO₃ in pores
Inputs
Cured brick, S. pasteurii culture, urea, a calcium source
Outputs
Calcium carbonate that fills pores and links grains
Purpose
Strengthen and seal the brick

Acoustic agglomeration

Intense sound waves make the air, and the particles in it, move back and forth. Small particles follow that motion closely while larger ones lag behind, so particles of different sizes move relative to each other and collide. Colliding particles can stick, and the clusters keep growing. Clusters are larger than the fine dust they began as, so they settle out or are caught in a filter more easily.

Aerolith uses ultrasound. The best frequency and intensity are still to be found.

  • Dusty air + ultrasound
  • Collisions
  • Clusters
  • Easier collection
Inputs
Dust-laden air, ultrasound
Outputs
Larger dust aggregates
Purpose
Collect the fine dust that normally escapes

Mechanochemical processing

Milling and grinding do more than make powder finer. The impacts crack mineral crystals, expose fresh surfaces and create defects and disordered regions. That raises the surface area and reactivity of the mineral, so it reacts with CO₂ faster and more completely than untreated mineral. In Aerolith this activation step prepares magnesium-bearing minerals for carbonation.

Milling uses energy, which we must count against the CO₂ captured.

  • Collected dust
  • Milling
  • Fresh reactive surfaces
  • Ready for CO₂
Inputs
Collected dust with reactive minerals (or added MgO), mechanical energy
Outputs
Activated mineral powder
Purpose
Prepare minerals for carbonation
