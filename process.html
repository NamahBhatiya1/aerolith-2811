How it works

From dust in the air to bricks and captured CO₂, step by step.

Build a brick as you scroll

Follow one batch of quarry dust from the air to a finished brick. Use the dots to jump between stages.

Collected dust

Dust, collected

Acoustic agglomeration clumps fine airborne dust so it can be gathered and separated. The collected quarry dust is the raw material for the bricks.

Mixed with sodium silicate

The dust is mixed with sodium silicate. Geopolymerisation turns the mixture into a binding material.

Moulded

The binding mixture is shaped in moulds, giving the bricks their form.

Cured

The moulded bricks are cured so the binder can set into a solid block.

Strengthened with MICP

Microbially induced calcium carbonate precipitation adds natural calcium carbonate to further strengthen the cured bricks.

From dusty air to bricks and carbonates

Bricks from dust

  1. Mix quarry dust with sodium silicate. Geopolymerisation forms the binder.
  2. Mould the mixture into bricks.
  3. Cure the bricks.
  4. Apply MICP. Natural calcium carbonate forms and further strengthens them.

Cleaner air from dust and CO₂

  1. Ultrasound waves bring fine airborne dust together by acoustic agglomeration.
  2. Collect and separate the larger dust aggregates.
  3. Activate the dust mechanically to expose reactive mineral surfaces.
  4. Let magnesium oxide react with CO₂ to form stable magnesium carbonate.
  5. Separate the carbonates with a filtration system. A metal-organic framework (MOF) is a possible extra to test.
