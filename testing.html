Experimental testing

Aerolith is at the idea stage. No bricks have been made and no tests have been run, so nothing here is a result. This is what we plan to measure and what would count as success.

Materials

Quarry dust

The raw material. Its minerals are checked first, because they decide how well the binder forms and what the dust can react with.

Sodium silicate

The alkaline activator for geopolymerisation. Its concentration and amount are variables to test.

Bacterial culture

Sporosarcina pasteurii, with urea and a calcium source, for MICP.

Reactive minerals

Magnesium-bearing minerals, or magnesium oxide (MgO) if the dust holds too little, for carbonation.

Test plan

Brick strength

How we test: Make cured bricks or test cubes with and without MICP, plus an untreated control. Crush them in a compression test and measure water absorption.

Success looks like: MICP bricks are measurably stronger than untreated ones, and the results can be compared with the standard that applies to masonry units.

Planned

Dust collection efficiency

How we test: Release a known mass of dust in a closed chamber, run it with ultrasound off and then on, and weigh what is collected. Compare particle sizes before and after.

Success looks like: A higher share of dust is collected, as larger aggregates, with ultrasound on, and the result repeats across runs.

Planned

Carbonate formation

How we test: Expose activated and untreated mineral powder to CO₂ and measure mass gain. Confirm the product with X-ray diffraction (XRD) or thermogravimetric analysis (TGA).

Success looks like: Carbonate is detected in the activated sample, with more CO₂ taken up than in the untreated control.

Planned

MICP inside the brick

How we test: Check that the bacteria survive the brick’s pH, then image sections of treated bricks with a scanning electron microscope (SEM).

Success looks like: Calcium carbonate crystals are visible in the pores of treated bricks and absent from the control.

Planned

Results will be added here once testing has been done.

Open questions

Which minerals react?

Does the quarry dust hold enough reactive minerals for CO₂ capture, or does magnesium oxide need to be added?

How strong are the bricks?

How strong do the geopolymer bricks get after curing, and how much does MICP add?

How well does ultrasound collect dust?

Which settings clump fine particles into aggregates that can actually be collected?

How do we separate the carbonates?

Is a filtration system enough, or is a metal-organic framework (MOF) the better route?
